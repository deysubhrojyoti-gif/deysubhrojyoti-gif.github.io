Chapter 01 — OriginsKIIT · Baja EV “Phoenix”

Start with loose parts

I studied electrical engineering at KIIT University, and my capstone set the pattern for my whole career. PHOENIX was a Baja-format electric vehicle my team carried from concept to competition: drivetrain, motor control, power electronics. No subsystem was somebody else's problem. There was no somebody else.

The vehicle mattered less than what it taught me. I want the whole machine on my bench: the electrical, the mechanical, the code, and the deadline. I've been allergic to narrow lanes ever since. Every chapter that follows is the same instinct, pointed at a bigger machine.

Chapter 02 — DisciplineHasbro · Brainchild · Michigan MSE

Instinct learns discipline

Hasbro gave my instinct its discipline. For five years I was a project engineer inside a global product machine, shipping NERF Fortnite, Rivals and Elite Stryke on fixed 30-week cycles. I owned BOM, PCB schematics in Autodesk EAGLE, embedded firmware for motor control, sensors and wireless, plus tooling and compliance in Teamcenter and Windchill. I cleared injection-molding, SMD and ultrasonic-welding bottlenecks with 100% on-time resolution. You solve those problems standing on a factory floor, not in a slide deck.

I also scaled a sourcing portfolio from $5M to $25M and redesigned 30+ SKUs for 20-40% cost reduction with Six Sigma and value-stream mapping. Then I upgraded on purpose: a master's in computer engineering at Michigan for the software depth, and a year as engineering manager at Brainchild Engineering, leading NPI programs for medical, automotive and consumer clients and putting LLM workflows into real engineering practice for the first time.

Chapter 03 — OwnershipToothsure · OralVac

Carry all of it

Co-founding Toothsure audited every skill I had. A Class I/II medical device doesn't care that you're a founder-led team. It demands a full ISO 13485/9001 quality system, Design History Files, DFMEA/PFMEA risk analysis, and FDA CDRH/CDER communications. I built those systems myself, and stood up dual USA-China supply chains covering molding, filling, packaging and logistics for four device SKUs: zero to production in 12 months at 65-70% gross margins, across OralVac, APEHK and Dentsim.

I wrote the software too: product websites and APIs for APEHK and Dentsim, built end to end in Node.js, plus a production LLM customer-support system that ran post-launch feedback on its own. That's my operating style in one line. Deploy AI across finance, legal, support and engineering so a small team executes like one three times its size. We raised close to $1M and were named a 2026 Startup to Watch.

Chapter 04 — IntelligenceChemical Mechanix · Now

The machine starts thinking

Today I'm Co-founder and Chief AI Operations Engineer at Chemical Mechanix, leading artificial intelligence systems for manufacturing and fintech. Hasbro taught me what a factory floor runs on. Toothsure taught me what regulated data costs. Now I own an AI platform end to end: the agents, the APIs, the economics, even the DNS. That last part is literal. I built and deployed chemicalmechanix.com and its APIs in Node.js, and I run the hosting, DNS and TLS underneath.

The product is a production multi-agent AI platform that turns unstructured manufacturing data into real-time financial intelligence. Agents monitor plant operations, assist operators live, and translate production into profitability metrics that replace month-end reconciliation. The architecture was a deliberate engineering call: ingestion, monitoring, operator-assist and financial translation run as separate, independently auditable agents, not one model behind a giant prompt. Every output is traceable; every failure debugs in isolation. Workloads route across model providers on measured quality, latency and per-call cost, with Jetson-class edge inference where data can't leave the building. This work is now the subject of a USPTO filing: MechanixScoreAI (US 19/830,658, filed October 2026).

My conviction about AI is deliberately unglamorous. It's a judgment amplifier, not a judgment replacement. I deploy agents where their output can be verified and grounded. Anything a model can't trace to real data, it shouldn't say. That's how you ship AI into businesses where a wrong number costs real money.

How I work

Character, in practice

Precision beats inflation

This site describes six patent filings as exactly what they are: engineering I did, credit that belongs to the named inventors. I run my whole career that way. State precisely where I stand, then let the work close the gap. Precision buys trust. Inflation gets found out.

Root cause or nothing

The discipline is the same in any system. I've traced failed injection-molded parts back through tooling and process windows, walked packaging defects up the value stream, and debugged production code the same way. When TLS renewals silently failed, every obvious fix was certificate-level; the real cause was split-brain DNS from an old migration. Plausible fixes treat symptoms. I dig until the explanation accounts for everything.

Own the whole machine

Firmware to injection molding at Hasbro. Quality systems to fundraising at Toothsure. Agent architecture to DNS at Chemical Mechanix. I don't hand off the parts I'd rather not learn. Those are usually where products die.

Lead by serving

I lead innovation teams as a servant leader: clear the blockers, take the unglamorous work, push the credit to the team. The customer sits at the head of the table. At Toothsure we shaped products around caregivers' real bedside workflow, not around what was easy to build.

Invention that scales

Creativity is the cornerstone of my work, but an idea only counts once it survives reality. I move fast at proof-of-concept, then add the DFM, quality gates and unit economics that make the winner sustainable at production scale. Six USPTO filings and four production platforms came from that loop.

Leverage, verified

I use AI aggressively. It's how one person ships four production platforms while owning product. But only where the feedback loop lets me verify the output. The judgment stays human. The machine is the leverage.
